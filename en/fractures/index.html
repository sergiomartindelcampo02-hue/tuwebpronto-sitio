Fractures: assessment and follow-up in Mexicali

A fracture needs a clear diagnosis, treatment suited to the type of injury and follow-up while the bone heals. We also review previously treated fractures that are still bothering you.

What are the common symptoms?

  • Intense pain at one point after a blow or a fall.
  • Swelling and bruising that appear quickly.
  • Difficulty or inability to move or bear weight.
  • Visible deformity of the area.
  • Persistent pain in a fracture that was already treated.

What may be causing the problem?

  • Falls from standing height, especially in older adults.
  • Traffic and work accidents.
  • Sports injuries.
  • Stress fractures from repeated loading.
  • Fragile bone, for example from osteoporosis.

How is it diagnosed?

A physical exam and X-rays confirm most fractures. In some cases a CT scan is needed to plan treatment. During follow-up, imaging is repeated to check healing.

What treatment options are there?

  • Immobilization with a cast, splint or brace.
  • Fracture reduction when the fragments are displaced.
  • Surgery with plates, nails or screws when the fracture type requires it.
  • Pain control and monitoring of bone healing.
  • Rehabilitation to regain mobility and strength.

When not to wait for an appointment

If there is exposed bone, obvious deformity, heavy bleeding, or the limb turns cold, pale or numb, go to an emergency room or call 911.

What to expect from your consultation

  • A clinical history focused on how the problem started and what it keeps you from doing.
  • A physical exam of the affected area.
  • Review of any imaging you already have, or orders for what is needed.
  • A clear explanation of the likely diagnosis and treatment options.
  • A plan with next steps and follow-up.

Frequently asked questions

Do all fractures need surgery?

No. Many fractures are treated with a cast, splint or brace. Surgery is considered when the fragments are displaced, the fracture extends into a joint or it is unstable.

Can you review a fracture that was treated elsewhere?

Yes. Bring your X-rays and the treatment report. We review how the bone is healing and explain the next steps.

Why is rehabilitation needed after a fracture?

While immobilized, you lose strength and mobility. Rehabilitation helps you regain them progressively once the bone allows it.
