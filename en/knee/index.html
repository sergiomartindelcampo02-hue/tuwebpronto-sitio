Knee pain and injuries in Mexicali

Your knee is involved in almost everything you do on your feet. When it hurts, swells or feels loose, it helps to know which structure is affected before choosing a treatment.

What are the common symptoms?

  • Pain going up or down stairs, or when standing up.
  • Swelling after walking, running or exercising.
  • A feeling that the knee buckles or gives way.
  • Painful clicking, or the knee locks and won't fully straighten.
  • Stiffness in the morning or after sitting for a while.

What may be causing the problem?

  • Meniscus tears, common after a sudden twist or from wear over the years.
  • Ligament injuries, such as the anterior cruciate ligament, typical in sports with pivoting or jumping.
  • Cartilage wear (osteoarthritis), which causes pain, stiffness and changes in knee shape.
  • Kneecap problems and tendinitis.
  • Fractures or after-effects of previous injuries.

How is it diagnosed?

Diagnosis starts with your history —how it began, what makes it worse, what you have already tried— and a physical exam of the knee. Depending on the case, X-rays may be ordered to look at bone and wear, or an MRI to assess the menisci, ligaments and cartilage. Imaging is always read alongside your symptoms.

What treatment options are there?

  • Rehabilitation and guided exercise to regain strength and mobility.
  • Medication for pain and inflammation, when appropriate.
  • Temporary changes in activity while the injury is managed.
  • Injections in selected cases.
  • Surgery, such as arthroscopy or ligament reconstruction, when the diagnosis calls for it.

When not to wait for an appointment

If after a blow the knee looks deformed, you cannot put weight on the leg or it swells quickly and severely, go to an emergency room.

What to expect from your consultation

  • A clinical history focused on how the problem started and what it keeps you from doing.
  • A physical exam of the affected area.
  • Review of any imaging you already have, or orders for what is needed.
  • A clear explanation of the likely diagnosis and treatment options.
  • A plan with next steps and follow-up.

Frequently asked questions

Does every knee pain need an MRI?

Not always. A clinical history, a physical exam and an X-ray often point to the diagnosis. An MRI is ordered when its result could change the treatment, for example when a meniscus or ligament injury is suspected.

Does a meniscus tear always need surgery?

No. Some meniscus tears are managed with rehabilitation, pain control and activity changes. Surgery is considered depending on the type of tear, the symptoms —such as locking— and how it affects your daily life.

When should I see a doctor about knee pain?

It is worth getting checked if the pain does not improve with relative rest, if the knee swells, locks or feels unstable, or if you are already giving up activities because of it.
